# PDF STRUCTURE TEMPLATE
## AWS Candidate Assessment & Interview Preparation Documents

**Last Updated:** May 12, 2026  
**Template Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Reusable structure for comprehensive candidate assessment documents  
**Target Roles:** Technical Project Manager, Implementation Manager, Program Manager positions at Amazon AWS

---

## OVERVIEW

This template defines the complete structure for generating professional, comprehensive candidate assessment documents. The structure is organized in 7 major sections that flow logically from executive summary through detailed analysis to actionable preparation guidance.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Comprehensive:** 7 major sections covering assessment, skills, gaps, and preparation
- **Professional:** Executive summary first, detailed analysis follows
- **Actionable:** Moves from assessment to concrete next steps
- **Interview-Ready:** Includes 10 STAR questions with complete answers
- **Word Count:** 12,000-18,000 words
- **Page Count:** 18-24 pages
- **Format:** DOCX (for formatting control) → PDF (final delivery)

## SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY: Your Candidacy Assessment

### Purpose
Provide immediate, clear answer to "How good a fit is this candidate?" - bottom line up front, with reasoning, alternative perspectives, and actionable steps.

### Structure

#### 1.1 Direct Answer (Bottom Line Up Front)
**Content Type:** Executive narrative  
**Length:** 150-250 words  
**Purpose:** Immediate, unambiguous assessment of candidacy

**Must Include:**
- Probability of success percentage (e.g., "65-72%")
- Primary strength (what makes them viable)
- Primary gap (what's the challenge)
- Hiring manager's likely concern (what they'll worry about)
- Your counter-argument (how the candidate overcomes the gap)
- Clear recommendation (pursue or proceed with caution)

**Example Structure:**
> "You are a [X-Y%] probability candidate with [strength] and [strength]. Your [X] years of [experience] directly match [number] of the [total] required competencies. However, your [gap name] is a significant gap that requires [mitigation approach]."

#### 1.2 How I Got There (Step-by-Step Reasoning)

**Content Type:** Detailed analysis with supporting tables  
**Length:** 400-600 words  
**Purpose:** Show the analytical work that led to the assessment

**Subsections:**

1. **1.2.1 Skill-by-Skill Analysis**
   - Format: Table with columns: Rank | Competency | Your Match | Evidence
   - Shows how each required skill was evaluated against resume
   - Include match level (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW)
   - Include specific evidence from resume

2. **1.2.2 Why This Assessment**
   - Narrative explanation of the ranking methodology
   - Why certain skills matter more than others
   - How the candidate's background maps to requirements

3. **1.2.3 The Biggest Vulnerability**
   - Name the primary gap explicitly
   - Explain why it matters for this specific role
   - Note whether it's disqualifying or addressable

#### 1.3 Alternative Perspectives (Other Ways to See It)

**Content Type:** Narrative with multiple angles  
**Length:** 400-500 words  
**Purpose:** Show how candidate's apparent weaknesses can be reframed as strengths

**Structure:** 2-3 alternative perspectives, each labeled (Perspective A, Perspective B, etc.)

**Example:**
- Perspective A: "Your lack of retail experience is disqualifying" → Reframe as "Your program management fundamentals apply directly regardless of context"
- Perspective B: "You've never done this type of work before" → Reframe as "You've successfully entered new domains multiple times"
- Perspective C: "You're overqualified in some areas" → Reframe as "Your depth in program management is exactly what this role needs"

#### 1.4 Practical Action Plan (What You Can Do Now)

**Content Type:** Actionable steps organized by timeline  
**Length:** 300-400 words  
**Structure:**
- IMMEDIATE (This Week)
- TIER 1 PRIORITY (Before Interview)
- TIER 2 (High Value)
- TIER 3 (Nice to Have)

**Must Include:**
- Specific, actionable tasks (not vague advice)
- Time estimates for each task
- Clear learning objectives
- Expected outcomes

#### 1.5 FINAL ASSESSMENT

**Content Type:** Summary narrative  
**Length:** 150-200 words  
**Includes:**
- Probability of success restated
- Key differentiator (why they're viable despite gaps)
- Bottom line recommendation

## SECTION 2: Top 10 Required Skills/Competencies (Ranked by Importance)

### Purpose
Extract the 10 most critical competencies from the job description, ranked by how essential they are for role success.

### Structure

#### 2.1 Introduction & Ranking Methodology
**Length:** 200-300 words  
**Explains:**
- How the 10 skills were selected from the JD
- Why these 10 matter most
- What "ranked by importance" means for this role
- How to interpret the ranking

#### 2.2 Ranked Skills Table

**Format:** Rank | Skill/Competency | Why It Matters for [Role Title]

**Example:**
| Rank | Competency | Why It Matters |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Complex Cross-Functional PM | Core responsibility; must orchestrate multiple teams and priorities |
| 2 | Customer-Facing PM | Role is "primary customer-facing" position; customer relationships are critical |
| ... | ... | ... |
| 10 | [Least Critical] | [Why this matters but ranks lowest] |

**Guidelines:**
- Rank 1-3: CRITICAL for role success
- Rank 4-7: HIGH importance
- Rank 8-10: MEDIUM to HIGH importance
- All 10 skills should be present in top performers; ranking indicates where to focus

## SECTION 3: Resume-to-JD Alignment Assessment

### Purpose
Create a detailed skill-by-skill comparison showing exactly how the candidate's background matches required competencies.

### Structure

#### 3.1 Skills Alignment Table

**Format:** Requirement | Match Level | Gap/Evidence | Timeline to Close

**Match Levels:**
- HIGH: Candidate clearly demonstrates this skill across multiple roles/projects
- MEDIUM: Candidate has some evidence; may need to develop in specific context
- LOW: Candidate lacks direct experience; significant learning required

**Evidence Requirements:**
- Quote specific resume accomplishments
- Include project context and results
- Name the role/company where skill was demonstrated
- Be specific, not generic

**Gap Analysis:**
- What's missing compared to JD requirement
- How significant is the gap
- Is it learnable or fundamental weakness
- Timeline to close (if learnable)

**Example Row:**
| Requirement | Match Level | Gap/Evidence | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complex Cross-Functional PM | HIGH | "Managed 3 teams, 18 engineers across 5 concurrent projects in 18 months" + "Led 7-month SAFe implementation with 21 engineers" = proven multi-team management | Already strong; no gap |
| Retail Operations Knowledge | LOW | ZERO retail experience. Background is entirely software/cloud/IT infrastructure. Zero store deployment experience. | 20-25 hours to learn fundamentals |

## SECTION 4: Critical Gaps & Strategic Positioning

### Purpose
Deep dive into candidate's vulnerabilities and how to proactively address them in interviews.

### Structure

#### 4.1 Red Flags (Address Proactively in Interview)

**Content Type:** Problem statement + Mitigation strategy  
**Length:** 300-400 words  
**Structure:** Gap Name | Why It Matters | How to Address

**Example:**
- **Gap:** "You're a software PM trying to manage retail store deployments"
- **Why It Matters:** JWO explicitly requires "experience with retail store deployments" and "understanding retail operational constraints"
- **How to Address:** Reframe program management fundamentals as universally applicable; show operational partnership experience; commit to rapid retail context learning

#### 4.2 Risk Mitigation (Comprehensive)

**Content Type:** Risk-by-risk mitigation strategies  
**Length:** 800-1,200 words  
**Structure:** Risk #1 | Mitigation Strategy (repeated for 3-5 risks)

**Each Risk Includes:**
1. Clear problem statement
2. Why hiring manager might worry about it
3. Candidate's counter-argument (reframing)
4. Concrete evidence supporting the reframe
5. Positioning language for interviews

**Example Risk:**
- **Risk #1:** "You're a software/cloud PM trying to manage retail store deployments"
  - **Mitigation:** Your cross-functional program management and operational partnership experience is actually an advantage. Here's why...
  - **Evidence:** Zendesk implementation across operations teams; AWS migrations affecting different operational contexts; process improvements with operations leaders
  - **Interview Positioning:** "My strength is in managing complex cross-functional programs with diverse operational stakeholders..."

#### 4.3 STRENGTHS: What You Bring (Your Competitive Advantage)

**Content Type:** Bulleted narrative  
**Length:** 300-400 words  
**Purpose:** Reframe candidate's actual strengths that align with role needs

**Structure:**
- List 8-12 key strengths with supporting evidence
- Explain how each strength applies to the target role
- Close with positioning statement

## SECTION 5: Amazon Leadership Principles & Interview Preparation

### Purpose
Map Amazon leadership principles to role; provide 10 STAR interview questions with complete answers.

### Structure

#### 5.1 Introduction & Leadership Principle Alignment

**Length:** 150-200 words  
**Explains:**
- Which Amazon leadership principles matter for this role (typically 5-8 of the 16)
- Why each principle matters
- How they'll come up in interviews

**Example:**
> "For Implementation Manager, JWO role, these leadership principles are most critical: Ownership (taking responsibility for customer success), Customer Obsession (putting customer needs first), Bias for Action (moving forward despite ambiguity), Dive Deep (understanding operational challenges), Earn Trust (building credibility with retail partners)..."

#### 5.2-5.11 Interview Questions 1-10

**Content Type:** STAR format with leadership principle mapping  
**Length Per Question:** 500-800 words

**Structure Per Question:**

```
QUESTION [X]: [Question Title]
Leadership Principles: [List 2-3 relevant principles]

Question: [Exact question text in italics]

STAR ANSWER:
  SITUATION: [2-3 paragraphs setting context]
  TASK: [1 paragraph explaining responsibility]
  ACTION: [3-5 paragraphs detailing what candidate did]
  RESULT: [2-3 paragraphs with quantified outcomes]

Why This Answer Works: [2-3 paragraphs connecting to role needs]
```

**Guidelines for STAR Answers:**

1. **Situation**
   - Set the scene: company, team size, business context
   - Introduce the challenge or complexity
   - Explain why this situation matters
   - Length: 2-3 paragraphs

2. **Task**
   - What was the candidate responsible for
   - Why was it important
   - What made it challenging
   - Length: 1 paragraph

3. **Action**
   - This is the longest section (3-5 paragraphs)
   - Show the decision-making process
   - Explain the approach/methodology
   - Include specific examples of what was done
   - Show leadership, not just execution
   - Include how they handled obstacles

4. **Result**
   - Quantified outcomes (numbers, metrics, percentages)
   - How it solved the initial problem
   - Broader impact or learnings
   - Length: 2-3 paragraphs

5. **Why This Answer Works**
   - Connect the answer to role requirements
   - Show how it demonstrates necessary skills
   - Explain why hiring manager will care
   - Position for this specific role, not generic PM role

**Question Selection Strategy:**

The 10 questions should cover:
- Complex program/project management (2-3 questions)
- Customer-facing work / stakeholder management (2-3 questions)
- Operational improvement/efficiency (1-2 questions)
- Handling ambiguity/change (1-2 questions)
- Leadership/influence (1 question)

**Leadership Principle Mapping:**

Each question should align with 2-3 of the identified leadership principles for the role. Candidates will be evaluated on which principles their stories demonstrate.

## SECTION 6: Strategic Recommendations & Action Plan

### Purpose
Transition from assessment to preparation: what candidate should do to prepare for the interview.

### Structure

#### 6.1 Overall Assessment

**Length:** 300-400 words  
**Includes:**
- Fit assessment percentage restated
- Probability of success
- Primary strength
- Primary challenge
- Hiring manager's question (what they're evaluating)
- What candidate needs to demonstrate

**Example:**
> "You have a genuine shot at this role. Your program management fundamentals, ability to manage cross-functional teams, and proven track record of delivering complex projects under tight timelines make you a viable candidate despite lacking retail experience. The retail/store deployment gap is real but addressable through rapid immersion and proving your PM fundamentals translate to retail context."

#### 6.2 Pre-Interview Preparation (Next 2-4 Weeks)

**Structure:** Tiered tasks by priority

**Tier 1: Critical (Do This First)**
- Task 1: Master [Domain] & [Role] Context
  - Specific activities (YouTube videos, articles, research)
  - What candidate should know
  - Time estimate: 20-25 hours
- Task 2: Craft Gap Answer
  - 60-second prepared response
  - Should address lack of [critical experience]
  - Structure: acknowledge gap + highlight strengths + show learning ability + commit to learning
  - Time estimate: 5-10 hours
- Task 3: Reframe Experience
  - Take 3 biggest achievements
  - Rewrite emphasizing transferable skills for this role
  - Time estimate: 10 hours

**Tier 2: High Value (Do This Second)**
- Task 1: Conduct Informational Interviews (10 hours)
- Task 2: Develop Probing Questions (5 hours)
- Task 3: Practice STAR Answers (8 hours)

**Tier 3: Nice to Have (If Time Permits)**
- Task 1: Leadership Principles Deep Dive (3-4 hours)
- Task 2: First 90 Days Plan (2-3 hours)

**Total Preparation Time:**
- Tier 1: 35-45 hours (critical foundation)
- Tier 2: 23-30 hours (interview readiness)
- Tier 3: 5-10 hours (polish)
- **Total: 63-85 hours** over 2-4 weeks

#### 6.3 Interview Day Strategy

**Subsections:**

1. **Opening: Own the Narrative**
   - Address elephant in the room directly
   - Provide 60-90 second opening statement
   - Show confidence about strengths
   - Honest acknowledgment of gap
   - Clear understanding of what role needs

2. **Middle: Tell Stories Strategically**
   - Guidance on which stories to lead with for different questions
   - When to use which STAR answer
   - How to adapt stories to specific questions
   - Transitions between stories

3. **Ending: Show Long-Term Thinking**
   - What probing questions to ask
   - How to position questions (showing knowledge, not just curiosity)
   - Final closing statement options

- Why each principle matters in this pahse
- How they'll come up in interviews

**Example:**

#### 7.2-7.6 Phone Screen Interview Questions 1-5

## FORMATTING GUIDELINES

### Headings
- **Document Title:** 28pt, bold, centered, AWS blue (#1f4788)
- **Section Title:** 18pt, bold, left-aligned, AWS blue (#1f4788)
- **Subsection Title:** 14pt, bold, left-aligned, AWS dark blue (#2d5aa8)
- **Sub-subsection Title:** 12pt, bold, left-aligned, gray (#666666)

### Body Text
- **Font:** Calibri or Arial
- **Size:** 11pt
- **Line Spacing:** 1.15
- **Paragraph Spacing:** 6pt before, 6pt after
- **Alignment:** Justified for paragraphs, left-aligned for lists

### Tables
- **Header Background:** AWS blue (#1f4788)
- **Header Text:** White, bold, 11pt
- **Row Background (Alternating):** Light gray (#f5f5f5)
- **Borders:** 1pt solid gray (#cccccc)
- **Cell Padding:** 6pt on all sides

### Emphasis
- **Critical Information:** Bold, red text (#dd0000)
- **Positive Information:** Bold, green text (#00aa00)
- **Caution/Risk:** Bold, orange text (#ff9800)
- **Questions/Examples:** Italic text

### Page Breaks
- After Executive Summary
- After Top 10 Skills
- After Resume Alignment
- After Critical Gaps
- After Interview Questions
