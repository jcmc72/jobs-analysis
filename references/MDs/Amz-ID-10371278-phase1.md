## STAR STORY SET 1: PROCESS STANDARDIZATION AT SCALE

### Question 1: "Tell me about a time you developed a standardized framework that had to work across different teams or business units with different needs. How did you make it stick?"

**SITUATION:**  
At Tecnosoftware (2020), I was managing two agile teams (14 engineers total), but the company was scaling. Leadership asked me to standardize agile practices as we grew to 30+ engineers across multiple projects. The problem: teams had their own ways of working, and they didn't want to lose autonomy.

**TASK:**  
My job was to:
1. Develop a framework that would standardize CORE practices (not everything)
2. Drive adoption across multiple autonomous teams
3. Maintain team effectiveness during the transition
4. Measure success with clear KPIs

**ACTION:**  
I didn't try to force uniformity. Instead, I created a **tiered framework**:

1. **Core Standards (Non-negotiable):**
   - Sprint length (2 weeks)
   - Definition of Done checklist
   - Daily standup format (15 min, 3 questions)
   - Retrospective cadence (bi-weekly)
   - Velocity tracking methodology

2. **Adaptable Elements (Teams choose):**
   - Tools (Jira vs Azure DevOps)
   - Specific meeting times
   - How ceremonies are facilitated
   - Monitoring dashboards

3. **Adoption Strategy:**
   - Led workshop with team leads (2 hours) explaining WHY each standard mattered
   - Ran 3-sprint coaching program (I attended daily standups, reviewed retros)
   - Established communities of practice (teams shared learnings weekly)
   - Used leading indicators: velocity, defect rate, team survey scores

4. **Measurement:**
   - Tracked adoption KPIs: % of teams meeting core standards (target: 100%)
   - Velocity improvement: 18% in Q3 after full adoption
   - Defect rate: stable (proving standardization didn't sacrifice quality)
   - Team satisfaction: +2 points on 10-point scale (from 6.5 to 8.5)
   - Decision quality: fewer scope creep issues, faster course corrections

**RESULT:**  
- ✅ 100% adoption across 30+ engineers within 3 months
- ✅ Velocity increased 18%
- ✅ Team satisfaction improved
- ✅ Process became organizational standard—other teams adopted by choice
- ✅ Became the hiring/onboarding standard for new engineers

**Leadership Principles Demonstrated:**
- **Ownership:** Took personal responsibility for adoption success, not just document creation
- **Think Big:** Connected standardization to strategic scaling
- **Earn Trust:** Built credibility by showing results, not imposing solutions
- **Simplify:** Made agile accessible to teams with varying experience

**Why This Works for GPS:**  
This is EXACTLY what GPS does—develop standards that work across different teams. This story shows you can:
- Create frameworks that are standardized yet flexible
- Drive adoption through leadership, not mandate
- Measure what matters
- Think strategically about standardization

## STAR STORY SET 2: PROCESS IMPROVEMENT & EFFICIENCY

### Question 2: "Describe a time when you identified a broken process and drove improvement. How did you know it was actually working?"

**SITUATION:**  
At Tecnosoftware (early 2020), the decision-making process for approving feature releases was taking 7 days—unacceptable for a fast-moving startup. Leadership asked me to improve the process without sacrificing quality or security controls.

**TASK:**  
1. Diagnose WHY the process was slow
2. Redesign it without removing necessary controls
3. Implement the new process with minimal disruption
4. Prove it worked with data

**ACTION:**  
I didn't assume I knew the problem. I did process mining:

1. **Diagnosis Phase (2 days):**
   - Shadowed actual decisions (not assumed workflow)
   - Discovered the breakdown:
     - 3 days: review/waiting for approvals
     - 2 days: unnecessary approval chains
     - 2 days: communication between stakeholders
   - Talked to stakeholders individually:
     - Security team: wanted 4 controls, we had 12
     - QA: needed production data, we were using sanitized data
     - Product: wanted a single voice on decisions

2. **Redesign (data-driven):**
   - Eliminated unnecessary approvals (8 → 1 decision maker)
   - Parallelized reviews (product and QA review simultaneously, not sequentially)
   - Automated communication (decision tracker updated automatically)
   - Kept all 4 critical security controls

3. **Implementation (phased to reduce risk):**
   - Phase 1: Eliminated unnecessary approvals → 7 days to 5 days (quick win)
   - Phase 2: Parallelized reviews + automated comms → 5 days to 3 days
   - Rolled back if KPIs degraded

4. **Measurement (obsessive tracking):**
   - Cycle time: 7 days → 5 days → 3 days (57% improvement) ✅
   - Defect rate: 0.8% before → 0.9% after (no degradation) ✅
   - Security incidents: 2/quarter before → 1/quarter after (improved) ✅
   - Team satisfaction with process: 4/10 → 8/10 ✅

**RESULT:**  
- ✅ 57% cycle time improvement
- ✅ Maintained/improved quality metrics
- ✅ Process became template for other approval workflows
- ✅ Team satisfaction with process nearly doubled

**Leadership Principles Demonstrated:**
- **Bias for Action:** Implemented changes despite uncertainty
- **Dive Deep:** Diagnosed before assuming
- **Ownership:** Took responsibility for outcomes, not just implementation
- **Simplify:** Removed complexity while maintaining safety

**Why This Works for GPS:**  
GPS's job is literally to simplify/standardize processes. This story shows you:
- Can diagnose root causes (not just symptoms)
- Understand that standardization can improve efficiency
- Measure what matters
- Balance standardization with flexibility

## STAR STORY SET 4: DRIVING ADOPTION & INFLUENCING WITHOUT AUTHORITY

### Question 4: "Tell me about a time you had to drive change across an organization where you didn't have direct authority. How did you get people to buy in?"

**SITUATION:**  
At Global Hitss (2018), I was brought in to implement SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) for 3 teams (21 engineers total) that had been doing their own version of Agile for years. The teams didn't want change—they thought their current process was fine.

**TASK:**  
1. Implement SAFe without alienating teams
2. Maintain velocity during the transition
3. Get genuine buy-in (not just compliance)

**ACTION:**  
I took a coaching approach, not a mandate approach:

1. **Phase 0: Listening (1 week)**
   - Interviewed each team about their current process
   - Identified what was working and what wasn't
   - Found common pain points (planning, dependencies, cross-team coordination)

2. **Phase 1: Making the Case (2 weeks)**
   - Presented data: where teams struggled, where they succeeded
   - Showed how SAFe addressed their pain points
   - Positioned SAFe as "evolution of what you're doing," not "replacement"
   - Let them shape implementation details

3. **Phase 2: Implementation (2 months)**
   - Trained all teams on SAFe together (built community)
   - Coached teams through first program increment
   - Attended ceremonies to answer questions
   - Adjusted based on feedback
   - Celebrated early wins

4. **Phase 3: Reinforcement (ongoing)**
   - Monthly retrospectives across all teams
   - Communities of practice for shared learning
   - Metrics showed improvements

**RESULT:**  
- ✅ Full SAFe implementation in 7 months
- ✅ Velocity increased (not decreased during transition)
- ✅ Teams voluntarily continued SAFe practices
- ✅ Teams requested additional SAFe training
- ✅ Framework became model for other departments

**Leadership Principles Demonstrated:**
- **Earn Trust:** Built credibility by listening first, not mandating
- **Simplify:** Made SAFe approachable and relevant
- **Bias for Action:** Moved forward despite initial resistance
- **Ownership:** Took responsibility for adoption success

**Why This Works for GPS:**  
GPS has to influence people they don't directly manage. This shows you can:
- Overcome resistance through genuine engagement
- Build credibility as a change agent
- Balance standardization with flexibility
- Measure and iterate on changes
