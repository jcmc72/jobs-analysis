## STAR STORY SET 2: CROSS-ORGANIZATIONAL COORDINATION

### Question 2: "Tell me about a time you had to coordinate across multiple departments or teams to get something complex done. How did you handle different priorities and cultures?"

**SITUATION:**  
At Contractor (2020), I led an AWS migration project that spanned three business units with completely different characteristics:

**The Context:**
- **Commercial unit:** Risk-averse, zero downtime tolerance, financial transactions at stake
- **Marketing unit:** Fast-moving, experimentation focus, willing to take risks
- **Support unit:** Process-oriented, change-averse, critical infrastructure steward
- Challenge: Each unit had different requirements, different risk tolerances, different timelines

**TASK:**  
1. Design migration approach that worked for all three (without compromising any)
2. Coordinate execution across teams without creating bottlenecks
3. Manage conflicting requirements and priorities
4. Keep all three units satisfied with progress

**ACTION:**  
I didn't try to force one approach. Instead, I created a **differentiated migration strategy**:

1. **Understanding Phase (2 weeks):**
   - Met with each business unit leadership
   - Understood their requirements, constraints, risk tolerance
   - Identified what could be standardized vs. what needed to be customized
   - Built relationship with technical leads in each unit

2. **Strategy Design:**
   - **Commercial:** Lift-and-shift approach (zero risk of data loss/downtime)
     - 3-month timeline (slower but safer)
     - Full rollback capability
     - Parallel running during transition
   - **Marketing:** Refactoring approach (could optimize during move)
     - 2-month timeline (faster, accepting some risk)
     - Microservices architecture opportunity
     - Could go faster because less risk-averse
   - **Support:** Phased approach (couldn't take systems down)
     - 4-month timeline (longest because critical infrastructure)
     - Incremental migration of services
     - Heavy testing required

3. **Coordination Mechanism:**
   - Monthly steering committee (all three units + leadership)
   - Weekly technical sync (all three tech leads)
   - Shared infrastructure (single AWS account, but separate VPCs)
   - Shared resource pool (one senior architect supporting all three)

4. **Managing Conflicts:**
   - When priorities clashed: escalated to steering committee
   - When technical approaches conflicted: found common ground (standardized on tagging, monitoring, security)
   - When schedules conflided: negotiated realistic timelines
   - Kept each unit updated on others' progress (transparency)

5. **Measurement:**
   - All three units delivered on their timelines
   - Commercial: Zero downtime (met requirement)
   - Marketing: Cost reduction 25% (refactoring worked)
   - Support: Zero critical incidents (phased approach worked)
   - Cross-unit learning: Other business units adopted similar approaches

**RESULT:**  
- ✅ All three units successfully migrated to AWS
- ✅ Zero downtime for commercial (achieved zero-tolerance requirement)
- ✅ 25% cost reduction for marketing (refactoring paid off)
- ✅ Zero critical incidents for support (safety maintained)
- ✅ Each unit satisfied with approach (tailored to their needs)
- ✅ Became case study for multi-unit project coordination

**Leadership Principles Demonstrated:**
- **Ownership:** Took responsibility for coordinating across org boundaries
- **Dive Deep:** Understood each unit's unique needs and constraints
- **Earn Trust:** Built credibility with each unit before coordinating
- **Simplify:** Made complex multi-unit migration clear and manageable
- **Customer Obsession:** Focused on each unit's specific needs

**Why This Works for Network Engineering:**  
Network Engineering coordinates across multiple teams (infrastructure teams, vendors, other departments). This shows you can:
- Understand different stakeholder perspectives
- Design solutions that work for multiple groups
- Coordinate without forcing uniformity
- Manage competing requirements
- Build trust across organizational boundaries

## STAR STORY SET 4: MANAGING DEPENDENCIES & VENDOR COORDINATION

### Question 4: "Tell me about a time you had to manage complex dependencies between teams or with external vendors. How did you keep things on track?"

**SITUATION:**  
At Contractor (2020-2022), I was managing infrastructure modernization that required close coordination between:
- Internal development teams (building features)
- Infrastructure team (provisioning AWS resources)
- Security team (approving infrastructure changes)
- AWS vendor (critical for capacity planning)

**The Problem:**
Any one of these could become a bottleneck. Security took 2 weeks to approve changes. AWS couldn't scale fast enough for our timeline. Dev and infrastructure weren't communicating effectively.

**TASK:**  
1. Accelerate infrastructure provisioning without sacrificing security
2. Align internal and external timelines
3. Prevent any team from becoming a bottleneck
4. Maintain team relationships (these are critical ongoing)

**ACTION:**  
I created a dependency management system:

1. **Mapping (Week 1):**
   - Documented all dependencies (what each team needed from others)
   - Built a timeline showing critical path
   - Identified high-risk dependencies (where delays cascade)
   - Ranked by impact (what could delay the project most)

2. **Communication (Ongoing):**
   - Weekly sync with all teams (everyone sees same view)
   - Monthly planning with AWS vendor (alignment on capacity)
   - Bi-weekly security reviews (approval process, not gatekeeper)
   - Real-time escalation for blockers (Slack channel, daily check)

3. **Process Improvement:**
   - **Security:** Moved from 2-week approval to 3-day review
     - Mechanism: Pre-review with security leads (get feedback early)
     - Formal review became confirmation, not discovery
     - Built trust with security by involving them earlier
   - **AWS Vendor:** Monthly capacity planning instead of ad-hoc
     - Forecasted needs 3 months out
     - Vendor could allocate resources in advance
     - Built partnership (win-win for both)
   - **Dev & Infra:** Weekly technical sync (aligning requirements)
     - Dev wrote infra requirements early
     - Infra could provision in advance
     - Prevented surprises

4. **Contingency Planning:**
   - For each critical dependency, developed backup plan
   - Example: If AWS couldn't scale, we had on-prem fallback
   - Example: If security approval delayed, we had pre-approved templates
   - Never exercised contingencies (prevention worked)

5. **Measurement:**
   - Mean time to provision: 2 weeks → 3 days
   - Security approval: 2 weeks → 3 days
   - On-time delivery despite dependency risks
   - Vendor satisfaction: maintained
   - Team satisfaction: appreciated clarity

**RESULT:**  
- ✅ Accelerated infrastructure provisioning by 85%
- ✅ Zero project delays due to dependencies
- ✅ Security approved 95% without iteration
- ✅ AWS vendor became proactive partner
- ✅ Teams appreciated transparent communication
- ✅ Became model for cross-team coordination

**Leadership Principles Demonstrated:**
- **Ownership:** Owned the dependency management, not blaming other teams
- **Bias for Action:** Made decisions to unblock teams
- **Dive Deep:** Understood what each team actually needed
- **Earn Trust:** Built relationships (not just processes)

**Why This Works for Network Engineering:**  
Network Engineering depends on many teams and vendors. This shows you can:
- Identify critical dependencies early
- Create communication mechanisms that prevent surprises
- Work effectively with vendors
- Solve problems (not just report them)
- Maintain relationships while driving results
